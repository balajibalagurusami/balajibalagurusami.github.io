1. Physical model → 3 degrees of freedom

The handwritten notes model a multi-storey building as three lumped masses connected by springs and dampers. The lateral floor motions are u₁, u₂, u₃.

Core idea: each mass moves independently, so a 3-mass model needs three displacement coordinates: u₁, u₂, u₃.
{u} = [ u₁   u₂   u₃ ]ᵀ

The mass model is lumped: each floor mass is concentrated at one coordinate. Springs represent storey stiffness; dashpots represent damping.

2. Free-body reasoning: relative motion matters

A spring or damper between two masses reacts to the difference in their motion. That is the key to every off-diagonal term later.

Spring between 1 and 2:   k₂(u₂ − u₁)
Damper between 1 and 2:   c₂(u̇₂ − u̇₁)
1Mass m₁: it is connected to ground by k₁,c₁ and to m₂ by k₂,c₂.
2Mass m₂: it feels interaction from both sides: the 1–2 pair and the 2–3 pair.
3Mass m₃: it only interacts with m₂ through k₃,c₃.

3. Write one equilibrium equation for each mass

The following is the cleaned algebraic form of the three handwritten mass equations.

1Mass 1
m₁ü₁ + c₁u̇₁ + k₁u₁ + c₂(u̇₁−u̇₂) + k₂(u₁−u₂) = F₁(t)
2Collect coefficients for mass 1
m₁ü₁ + (c₁+c₂)u̇₁ − c₂u̇₂ + (k₁+k₂)u₁ − k₂u₂ = F₁(t)
3Mass 2
m₂ü₂ + c₂(u̇₂−u̇₁) + k₂(u₂−u₁) + c₃(u̇₂−u̇₃) + k₃(u₂−u₃) = F₂(t)
4Mass 3
m₃ü₃ + c₃(u̇₃−u̇₂) + k₃(u₃−u₂) = F₃(t)
Practical reading: diagonal terms collect everything attached to that mass; off-diagonal terms are the negative coupling to a neighbouring mass.

4. Assemble the matrix equation

[M]{ü} + [C]{u̇} + [K]{u} = {F(t)}

Mass matrix [M]

m₁00
0m₂0

Damping matrix [C]

c₁+c₂−c₂0
−c₂c₂+c₃−c₃
0−c₃c₃

Stiffness matrix [K]

k₁+k₂−k₂0
−k₂k₂+k₃−k₃
0−k₃k₃

Vectors

{u} = [u₁ u₂ u₃]ᵀ    {u̇} = [u̇₁ u̇₂ u̇₃]ᵀ    {ü} = [ü₁ ü₂ ü₃]ᵀ
{F(t)} = [F₁(t) F₂(t) F₃(t)]ᵀ
Memory pattern: diagonal = “sum of elements touching this DOF”; neighbour coupling = negative connector value; no direct connection = 0.

5. The four vibration cases from Lecture 09

Tap a case. The general MDOF equation is simplified by switching damping and external force on/off.

① Undamped free vibration
C = 0 and F(t) = 0
[M]{ü} + [K]{u} = 0
② Damped free vibration
F(t) = 0
[M]{ü} + [C]{u̇} + [K]{u} = 0
③ Undamped forced vibration
C = 0
[M]{ü} + [K]{u} = {F(t)}
④ Damped forced vibration
Nothing removed
[M]{ü} + [C]{u̇} + [K]{u} = {F(t)}
No damping and no continuing external load. Motion continues because of the initial disturbance and the interaction of mass and stiffness.

7. Quick knowledge check

Q1. Why does k₂ appear as −k₂ in positions (1,2) and (2,1) of [K]?
The connector force depends on u₁−u₂ or u₂−u₁. When coefficients are collected, the neighbour displacement enters with a negative sign.
Q2. Which case gives [M]{ü} + [K]{u} = 0?
Free means {F(t)} = 0. Undamped means [C] = 0. Both disappear, leaving [M]{ü} + [K]{u} = 0.
Q3. What sits on the diagonal K₂₂?
Mass 2 is connected to spring k₂ on one side and k₃ on the other. The diagonal collects both: k₂+k₃.
