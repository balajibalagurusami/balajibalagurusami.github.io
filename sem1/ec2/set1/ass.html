Mid-Semester Test (EC-2 Regular Set-2), 19 Sep 2026 - step-by-step worked solutions for Questions 1 to 3.

Important: Q2 has been solved from the actual exam sketch, not copied from the similar textbook examples. The exam frame has pin supports at A and F, the 40 kN horizontal load acts at B, and the 80 kN vertical load acts at D. These differences change the collapse mechanism.

Notation: the symbol × means ordinary scalar multiplication throughout these calculations; no vector cross product is used.

Question 1 - Classification and strong-axis bending capacity

Full question: A I-section beam is fabricated with plates of following dimensions flanges 410 mm × 20 mm and web 1400 mm × 15 mm. Classify flanges, web and the section. Determine the bending capacity of the beam about its strong axis, Grade of steel Fe410. [30M]

Given: fabricated I-section; flange plates 410 mm × 20 mm, web plate 1400 mm × 15 mm, steel Fe410.

Step 1 - Material values and epsilon

For Fe410 / E250 steel, take yield stress fy = 250 MPa for these plate thicknesses and γm0 = 1.10.

Formula: ε = √(250 / fy)
Substitute: ε = √(250 / 250) = 1.0

Reference: IS 800:2007, Table 2 (section classification) and Table 5 (partial safety factor).

Step 2 - Classify the flange

Each flange outstand is measured from the web face to the free edge:

Formula: b = (B - tw) / 2
Substitute: b = (410 - 15) / 2 = 197.5 mm
Formula: b / tf
Substitute: 197.5 / 20 = 9.875

Because this is a fabricated/welded I-section, use the welded outstand limits: 8.4ε (plastic), 9.4ε (compact), and 13.6ε (semi-compact).

9.4 < 9.875 ≤ 13.6 ⇒ flange is SEMI-COMPACT
Step 3 - Classify the web

Under major-axis bending, the web is an internal element with stress changing from compression to tension.

Formula: d / tw
Substitute: 1400 / 15 = 93.33

For a web in bending, the limits are 84ε (plastic), 105ε (compact), and 126ε (semi-compact).

84 < 93.33 ≤ 105 ⇒ web is COMPACT

The section class is governed by the least favourable element. Therefore the whole I-section is SEMI-COMPACT.

Step 4 - Section moduli about the strong axis

The section is doubly symmetric, so the plastic neutral axis is at mid-depth.

Formula: D = d + 2 × tf
Substitute: D = 1400 + 2 × 20 = 1440 mm

Top flange:

Formula: Af = B × tf
Substitute: Af = 410 × 20 = 8200 mm²
Formula: yf = D/2 - tf/2
Substitute: yf = 720 - 10 = 710 mm

Half web:

Formula: Aw/2 = tw × (d/2)
Substitute: Aw/2 = 15 × 700 = 10,500 mm²
Formula: yw = d/4
Substitute: yw = 1400/4 = 350 mm
Formula: Zp = 2[Afyf + Aw/2yw]
Substitute: Zp = 2[(8200 × 710) + (10,500 × 350)] = 18.994 × 106 mm³
Step 5 - Elastic section modulus governing a semi-compact section
Formula: Ix = 2[(B × tf3 / 12) + Afyf2] + (tw × d3 / 12)
Substitute: Ix = 2[(410 × 20³ / 12) + (8200 × 710²)] + (15 × 1400³ / 12)
Result: Ix = 11.6978 × 109 mm⁴
Formula: Ze = Ix / (D/2)
Substitute: Ze = 11.6978 × 10⁹ / 720 = 16.2469 × 106 mm³

For a semi-compact section, IS 800 uses the elastic section modulus for the cross-section bending strength (equivalently βb=Ze/Zp).

Step 6 - Design bending strength

With no unbraced length or lateral restraint data given, the normal exam assumption is a laterally supported beam; therefore lateral-torsional buckling is not evaluated.

Formula: Md = Ze × fy / γm0
Substitute: Md = 16.2469 × 10⁶ × 250 / 1.10 = 3.6925 × 10⁹ N·mm
Md = 3692.5 kN·m ≈ 3.69 MN·m
Q1 answer: Flange = semi-compact; web = compact; section = semi-compact. Strong-axis design bending capacity ≈ 3693 kN·m (laterally supported assumption).

Question 2 - Portal frame plastic moment by virtual work

Full question: A portal Frame ABCDEF of uniform cross section is subjected to a 40 kN horizontal load and a vertical load of 80 kN is at D. Using principle of virtual work determine the plastic moment. [40M]

Actual exam geometry: A and F are pin supports. AB = 6 m, BC = 4 m, CD = 3 m, DE = 3 m, EF = 6 m. A 40 kN horizontal load acts at B and an 80 kN downward load acts at D.

Why the textbook picture cannot be copied: changing a fixed support to a pin, changing a load point, or changing a column height changes the number/location of plastic hinges and the virtual displacements. Here the governing mechanism is a sway mechanism with plastic hinges at B and E.
Step 1 - Identify the governing mechanism

The real pins at A and F already permit rotation without plastic work. Introduce plastic hinges at B and E. Then:

• member AB rotates about A;
• the rigid part BCDE translates horizontally without rotation;
• member EF rotates about F.

Let the small rotation of AB and EF be θ. Because AB and EF are each 6 m long, the horizontal translation of B, C, D and E is:

Formula: δ = L × θ
Substitute: δ = 6 × θ
Step 2 - External virtual work

The 40 kN force at B moves through the horizontal virtual displacement 6θ:

Formula: Wext,H = PH × δH
Substitute: Wext,H = 40 × (6 × θ) = 240 × θ kN·m

Point D translates horizontally only in this mechanism, so its vertical displacement is zero. Therefore the 80 kN vertical load does no virtual work:

Formula: Wext,V = PV × δV
Substitute: Wext,V = 80 × 0 = 0
Total: Wext = 240 × θ
Step 3 - Internal plastic work

There are two plastic hinges, B and E. Each undergoes a relative plastic rotation θ because the middle block BCDE does not rotate.

Formula: Wint = Σ(Mp × θhinge)
Substitute: Wint = Mp × θ + Mp × θ = 2 × Mp × θ
Step 4 - Apply the principle of virtual work
Formula: Wext = Wint
Substitute: 240 × θ = 2 × Mp × θ
Mp = 120 kN·m
Step 5 - Static-theorem verification (important check)

A one-parameter equilibrium moment field can be written using the horizontal reaction at F, X = Fx.

MB = 240 + 6X;   MC = 240 + 10X;   MD = 240 + 8X;   ME = 6X

Choose X = -20 kN:

SectionMoment (kN·m)
B+120
C+40
D+80
E-120

Every section satisfies |M| ≤ 120 kN·m, while B and E simultaneously reach ±Mp. This lower-bound check confirms that the virtual-work result is the exact collapse plastic moment, not merely a possible mechanism value.

Q2 answer: Required plastic moment of the uniform frame section, Mp = 120 kN·m. Governing plastic hinges: B and E. The 80 kN load at D has zero virtual work in this governing sway mechanism.

Question 3 - Plastic section modulus of ISMB 225

Full question: Determine the plastic section modulus about major axis and minor axis, the section dimensions D = 225 mm, B = 110 mm, thickness of flange 11.8 mm and thickness of web = 6.5 mm. (ISMB 225 @ 306.07 N/m) [30M]

Given: D = 225 mm, B = 110 mm, tf = 11.8 mm, tw = 6.5 mm. For the hand calculation, idealise the section as three rectangles (fillets ignored), matching the common textbook method.

Step 1 - Clear web depth
Formula: d = D - 2 × tf
Substitute: d = 225 - 2 × 11.8 = 201.4 mm

Because the idealised section is symmetric about both axes, the plastic neutral axes coincide with the centroidal z-z and y-y axes.

Step 2 - Major-axis plastic modulus Zpz

Use the top half and double it.

Top flange:

Formula: Af = B × tf
Substitute: Af = 110 × 11.8 = 1298 mm²
Formula: yf = D/2 - tf/2
Substitute: yf = 225/2 - 11.8/2 = 106.6 mm

Half web:

Formula: Aw/2 = (d/2) × tw
Substitute: Aw/2 = (201.4/2) × 6.5 = 654.55 mm²
Formula: yw = d/4
Substitute: yw = 201.4/4 = 50.35 mm
Formula: Zpz = 2[Af × yf + Aw/2 × yw]
Substitute: Zpz = 2[(1298 × 106.6) + (654.55 × 50.35)]
Zpz = 342.65 × 10³ mm³ ≈ 342.6 cm³
Step 3 - Minor-axis plastic modulus Zpy

For the y-y axis, take the right half and double it. In that half-section:

Two half-flanges together have area:

Formula: Af,R = 2 × (B/2) × tf
Substitute: Af,R = 2 × (110/2) × 11.8 = 1298 mm²

Their centroid is 110/4 = 27.5 mm from the y-y axis.

Half of the web has area:

Formula: Aw,R = (tw/2) × d
Substitute: Aw,R = (6.5/2) × 201.4 = 654.55 mm²

Its centroid is 6.5/4 = 1.625 mm from the y-y axis.

Formula: Zpy = 2[Af,R × (B/4) + Aw,R × (tw/4)]
Substitute: Zpy = 2[(1298 × 27.5) + (654.55 × 1.625)]
Zpy = 73.52 × 10³ mm³ ≈ 73.52 cm³
Step 4 - Steel-table comparison

The idealised rectangular calculation omits root/toe fillets. Published ISMB 225 steel-table data give a major-axis plastic modulus of about 348.27 × 10³ mm³ when the real rolled profile geometry is included. Therefore:

Hand calculation (fillets ignored): Zpz ≈ 342.65 × 10³ mm³
Tabulated rolled section: Zpz ≈ 348.27 × 10³ mm³

The small difference is expected. Since the exam explicitly supplies D, B, tf, and tw, the dimensional hand-calculation values are the appropriate worked answer.

Q3 answer (idealised rectangular section): Zpz ≈ 342.65 × 10³ mm³ and Zpy ≈ 73.52 × 10³ mm³.

References and exam-use notes

1. IS 800:2007 - General Construction in Steel: Code of Practice. Used for section classification limits, material partial factor and bending strength format.

2. IS 808 / SP 6 (Part 1) - rolled steel section dimensions/properties. ISMB 225 dimensions and tabulated major-axis plastic modulus are consistent with published steel-table reproductions.

3. The calculation in Q3 intentionally follows the idealised three-rectangle method because the question supplies D, B, tf and tw. Fillet-inclusive tabulated values are shown only as a check.

4. For Q1, the bending strength is calculated as a laterally supported section because the question gives no unbraced length or lateral restraint information. If the examiner intends a laterally unsupported beam, additional LTB data are required.

Final answers at a glance:
Q1: semi-compact section; Md ≈ 3693 kN·m.
Q2: Mp = 120 kN·m, hinges at B and E.
Q3: Zpz ≈ 342.65 × 10³ mm³; Zpy ≈ 73.52 × 10³ mm³.
